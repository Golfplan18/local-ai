**Structural definition.** A bow_tie is a RISK-family diagram with bilateral symmetry around ONE center node, `hazard_event` (the "top event" / knot). LEFT = `threats[]` (initiating causes), each carrying `preventive_controls[]` on the threat→event pathway. RIGHT = `consequences[]` (outcomes), each carrying `mitigative_controls[]` on the event→consequence pathway. The symmetry IS the artifact — it visualizes prevention vs. mitigation as mirror halves.

**Hard side-rules (Critical/auto-block if violated).** Preventive control `type` ∈ {eliminate, reduce, detect} ONLY. Mitigative control `type` ∈ {reduce, recover, contain} ONLY. `recover`/`contain` on the left or `eliminate`/`detect` on the right = "controls on wrong side" → render blocked. `reduce` is the only type legal on both sides; use it deliberately. Required: `hazard_event.label`; ≥1 `threats`; ≥1 `consequences`; every threat needs `id`+`label`+`preventive_controls`; every consequence needs `id`+`label`+`mitigative_controls`; every control needs `id`+`label`+`type`. `effectiveness` (H/M/L) and `severity` (H/M/L) are optional but add the fragility-audit signal — include them.

**When THIS type vs siblings.** Bow_tie when the prompt is one hazard with BOTH causes and consequences AND named barriers. If only causes feed an outcome → fishbone or causal_dag (no barriers, no right half). If it's a sequential failure flow → flowchart. The bow_tie's distinguishing claim is the barrier layer between cause and event and between event and outcome.

**Failure modes from the judge (defeat these).** (1) LOPSIDED render: 9 threats vs 1 consequence collapses the right half. Keep counts balanced (e.g. 3/3) and legible — populate the consequence side as richly as the threat side. (2) BARRIER OVERLAP: too many controls stack into an unreadable column over the knot. Cap ~2 controls per threat/consequence; keep labels short. (3) WRONG-SIDE controls (the one Critical block). (4) The minimal fixture's `semantic_description` is COPIED bar-chart boilerplate ("Bar chart of four categories…", "C is the largest at 42") — that is the canonical garble; describe THIS bow-tie. Use `type_specific` with threat/consequence/control counts (per §8.1). Add `escalation_factors[]` (a `from_control_id` pointing at a real control id) to encode correlated-failure fragility — high value in this mode.
RENDER LEGIBILITY: keep it to ≤3 threats and ≤3 consequences with ≤2 controls each. `escalation_factors` now render cleanly (the renderer's escalation link no longer trips the overlap suppressor) — use them for correlated-failure fragility, but keep them few. The symmetric threat→hazard→consequence spine is the deliverable.
