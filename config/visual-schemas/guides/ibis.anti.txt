The canonical wrong output is a thin binary map that reskins a pro_con tree: one question, two ideas (or one idea with a pro column and a con column), no sub-issue, no third rival position — the judge scores this "under-built / shows a binary dialectic rather than a many-positions issue map." Its degenerate cousins are (a) a prose dialectical essay with NO spec graph emitted at all (lowest-scoring lane), and (b) an over-corrected ~11:1 illegible-wide layout from cramming in 8 positions. The one rule that prevents all three: ALWAYS emit a nodes+edges graph with 3-4 `idea` nodes each `responds_to` the `question` and each carrying ≥1 `pro` (`supports`) and ≥1 `con` (`objects_to`), plus exactly one sub-`question` via `questions` — rich enough to beat the binary trap, bounded enough to stay legible.
