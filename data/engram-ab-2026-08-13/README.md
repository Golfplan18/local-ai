# A/B evidence — permanent-note standard port (ora `59c90915`)

Measurement record behind PR #295, which ported six rules from
`scripts/engram-migration/rewrite_prompt.md` into the two note producers.
Run 2026-08-13. Kept because the PR cites these numbers and the scratchpad
they were produced in does not survive the session.

The calibration record those rules came from is preserved at
`vault/Projects/Ora/Reference — Permanent Note Writer Calibration Record.md`.

## What was run

**`ab_extract.py` → `ab_results.json`.** 16 cleaned pairs sampled
deterministically (seed 20260813) from `~/Documents/Commercial AI archives`,
`private`-tagged pairs excluded, each ≥1200 chars. Every pair run twice through
the same production model (`xiaomi/mimo-v2.5-pro`, OpenRouter, temp 0) — once
with the prompt at `HEAD`, once with the ported prompt. OLD minted 33 notes,
NEW 27.

**`judge_ab.py` → `judge_ab_results.json`.** All 60 notes pooled, arm labels
stripped, order shuffled (seed 4242), judged one at a time with the source pair
attached by Opus 5 — a different model family from the producer. Six criteria
plus an overall SOUND/DEFECT. 8 judge parse errors, 5 old / 3 new, proportional
to arm size.

| criterion | old | new | |
|---|---|---|---|
| conversion | 25% | 50% | +25pp |
| qualifications | 50% | 83% | +33pp |
| generalization | 75% | 88% | +12pp |
| domain | 93% | 96% | +3pp |
| no imported term | 86% | 96% | +10pp |
| actors | 71% | 88% | +16pp |
| **SOUND overall** | **18%** | **50%** | **+32pp** |

**`retest_errors.py`.** Two pairs failed to parse in the NEW arm on the first
run. Re-run 3× per arm: both parse 3/3 on both arms. Transient provider flake,
not prompt length.

## Rubric sanity check — read the scope before citing it

**`calibrate_rubric.py` → `calibration_results.json`.** Imports `RUBRIC`
verbatim from `judge_ab.py`, runs it over the 80-case benchmark at
`~/engram-work/.migration/judge_benchmark80_blind.json`, compares to
`judge_benchmark80_labels.json`. 69 scored, 11 dropped on parse errors
(9 SOUND / 2 DEFECT — proportional to the full set, so no skew).

Agreement with the labels: **61/69 = 88%**.

**This does not validate the +32pp.** The labels were generated by an Opus
pairwise judging pass, not by a human; the vault owner's own scorer says it
measures agreement with Opus, not correctness. Running an Opus rubric against
Opus-generated labels largely measures whether two Opus rubrics agree. The
yardstick carries the same bias as the instrument.

Two further limits on the number itself:

- The benchmark is 70 SOUND / 10 DEFECT. A rubric that answered SOUND to
  everything would also score 88%. Chance-corrected, **Cohen's kappa = 0.54**
  — moderate, not strong.
- The benchmark cases are notes rewritten from *source notes*; this rubric was
  written to judge notes extracted from *conversation pairs*. Applying it here
  is slightly out of its native context.

What it does establish: the rubric is not wildly miscalibrated, and it is not
rubber-stamping — it caught 6 of the 8 label-DEFECT cases (75%) while agreeing
on 55 of 61 label-SOUND (90%). Where the two differ it is stricter (6 of 8
disagreements), mostly refusing titles that neutrally restate a mechanism
without a conversion or a named cost.

## What the port's evidence actually rests on

Not the judge. Two judge-independent artifacts, both readable directly in
`ab_results.json`:

1. **Three pairs where NEW minted nothing and OLD minted two each.** All six of
   those OLD notes were independently judged DEFECT — platitudes and procedure
   restatements ("Effective application frameworks balance clear guidance with
   creative flexibility"). The lower note count is platitude suppression, not
   lost content.
2. **The de-fanging case** (`2025-06-13_07-56_objections-sound-like-bullshit.md`).
   OLD minted three near-tautologies that each dropped the source's conversion.
   NEW found it: *"When a reform redistributes advantage, incumbents reframe the
   redistribution as a system defect."*
