# Cooperation

## Trigger

Invoked from within game-theoretic-analysis, alliance-design, and repeated-interaction modes when an analyst is evaluating whether and how to cooperate with another party whose interests partially overlap and partially conflict. The host mode supplies the structure of the interaction (one-shot vs. repeated, payoff matrix or its qualitative analog, party identities and stakes); the lens supplies the conditions under which conditional cooperation outperforms both pure defection and pure altruism, plus the strategy template (signal-reciprocate-forgive-be-clear) that produces stable mutual gain.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

In repeated interactions with partial interest overlap, mutual cooperation produces better aggregate outcomes than mutual defection — but cooperation requires trust, and trust requires mechanism. The winning strategy is neither pure selfishness nor pure altruism but conditional cooperation: open generously, reciprocate behavior received, forgive occasional defections to prevent retaliation spirals, and communicate the rules so the other party can predict and trust them.

### Mechanism

Three structural conditions enable cooperation to dominate. Repetition: when the game has a future, future payoffs from continued cooperation outweigh present payoffs from defection. Reciprocity: matching behavior makes defection costly and cooperation rewarding within the relationship. Clarity: when each party can predict the other's response rule, both can rationally choose cooperation. Without any one of these conditions — when the game is one-shot, when behavior cannot be reciprocated, or when the strategy is opaque — defection becomes the rational choice and the cooperative equilibrium collapses.

### Applicability Conditions

- The interaction is repeated or has a credible future.
- The parties have overlapping but non-identical interests (pure-conflict or pure-aligned games are different lenses).
- Behavior is observable enough that reciprocity is feasible.
- A clear strategy can be communicated, by stated rule or by consistent demonstration.

### Common Misapplications

- Treating cooperation as morally required regardless of game structure (cooperation in a one-shot game with a defecting partner is exploitable, not virtuous).
- Confusing forgiveness (occasional, signal-based) with permissiveness (continuous, regardless of partner behavior); permissiveness invites exploitation.

### Related Models

- **Prisoner's Dilemma** — the canonical structure where cooperation is at risk; cooperation is the lens that resolves it under repetition.
- **Tit-for-Tat** — the specific strategy implementation; cooperation is the broader lens.
- **Reciprocity** — the underlying social mechanism on which conditional cooperation runs.

## Application Steps

1. Determine whether the game is repeated; if it is one-shot, cooperation strategies do not apply in their standard form.
2. Open with cooperation to signal willingness and to test the partner's response.
3. Reciprocate: match cooperation with cooperation and defection with proportionate, finite consequences.
4. Forgive occasional or accidental defections to avoid retaliation spirals that destroy the long-term relationship.
5. Communicate the strategy explicitly so the partner can predict the response and choose accordingly.

## Detection Signals

- Two or more parties have overlapping interests but also temptation to defect.
- The relationship or market interaction will repeat over time.
- A prisoner's-dilemma structure is visible (individual incentives conflict with collective benefit).
- Trust is being built between parties with competing interests, and the design of the trust mechanism matters.
- Partnership, alliance, or team-norm decisions are being made.

## Critical Questions

- Is the game actually repeated, or has the analyst assumed repetition that does not hold? Misjudging this collapses the strategy.
- Is the partner's behavior observable enough to reciprocate? If observation lags, reciprocity becomes noisy and the strategy degrades.
- Has the strategy been communicated, or is it being run silently? Silent cooperation cannot build the trust the lens requires.
- Are the forgiveness conditions defined in advance? Without them, occasional partner defections trigger escalation that destroys the relationship.

## Common Failure Modes

- **One-shot misapplication** — Detection signal: a cooperation strategy is invoked in a single-encounter setting with no future. Correction: switch to one-shot game analysis (defection is rational unless other constraints apply).
- **Permissive forgiveness** — Detection signal: the analyst forgives every partner defection without reciprocating, expecting trust to build. Correction: forgiveness is signal-based and finite; structural permissiveness invites exploitation.
- **Opaque strategy** — Detection signal: the cooperator never communicates the rule, expecting the partner to infer it. Correction: explicit communication is part of the strategy; without it, the partner cannot rationally cooperate.
