# Iyengar Episodic Thematic

## Trigger

Invoked when a frame or propaganda audit needs to assess whether a public issue is being framed through isolated cases or broader structures. The host mode supplies the media artifact, claim, or narrative; the lens supplies Iyengar's episodic/thematic distinction and attribution-of-responsibility check.

## Core Structure

An **episodic frame** presents an issue through a concrete event, person, anecdote, or case. A **thematic frame** presents the issue through trends, institutions, background conditions, statistics, or policy context. The frame influences who audiences hold responsible.

Episodic framing often pushes responsibility toward individuals. Thematic framing makes structural, institutional, and policy responsibility more visible. Neither form is automatically wrong; the analytical question is what each makes easier or harder to see.

## Application Steps

1. Identify the focal unit of the artifact: individual case, event, pattern, institution, or trend.
2. Classify the dominant frame as episodic, thematic, or mixed.
3. Ask who or what the frame makes responsible.
4. Identify what context is absent because of the frame choice.
5. Test whether switching frame type would change the implied remedy or blame.
6. Return the frame classification and responsibility effect.

## Detection Signals

- A policy issue is told through a single vivid victim, culprit, hero, or anecdote.
- A broad social problem is presented without trend or institutional context.
- Responsibility appears individualized despite structural background conditions.
- The analysis needs to distinguish humanizing example from context-erasing anecdote.

## Critical Questions

- Is the issue represented as a case or as a pattern?
- Who becomes responsible under this frame?
- What institutions, incentives, or background rates disappear?
- Is the episodic case representative or exceptional?
- Would a thematic frame imply a different remedy?
- Does the thematic frame erase morally relevant individual experience?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Anecdote dismissal** - Detection: episodic evidence is rejected solely because it is episodic. Correction: ask whether the case is representative and what it reveals.
- **Structure laundering** - Detection: thematic framing abstracts away concrete harm. Correction: preserve lived consequences while adding context.
- **Responsibility shortcut** - Detection: blame is inferred without mapping the frame's responsibility mechanism. Correction: state how the frame shifts attribution.
- **False binary** - Detection: mixed artifacts are forced into one category. Correction: identify which sections are episodic and which are thematic.
