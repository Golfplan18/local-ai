# Senge System Archetypes

## Trigger

Invoked when a systems mode needs to recognize recurring feedback-loop patterns behind persistent or surprising behavior. The host mode supplies the observed system behavior and causal map; the lens supplies Senge's archetype catalog so the analyst can identify the loop structure and the characteristic intervention.

## Core Structure

System archetypes are reusable dynamic patterns. They should be used only when the loop structure is present, not as labels pasted onto a narrative.

### Limits to Growth

A reinforcing loop drives growth until a balancing loop created by a constraint slows or reverses it. The intervention is to identify and relieve the limiting condition, not to push harder on the growth loop.

### Shifting the Burden

A symptomatic fix reduces pressure in the short term while weakening the capacity for a fundamental fix. The intervention is to reduce dependence on the symptomatic fix and build the fundamental capability.

### Fixes That Fail

A quick fix improves the symptom now but creates delayed side effects that worsen the original problem. The intervention is to map the delayed consequence and avoid the fix unless the side effect can be controlled.

### Eroding Goals

Pressure to close a performance gap is resolved by lowering the goal rather than improving capability. The intervention is to preserve the standard and address the capability gap directly.

### Escalation

Two actors treat each other's defensive move as a threat and respond in kind, producing a reinforcing arms race. The intervention is to interrupt the comparison loop and create credible de-escalation.

### Success to the Successful

Early advantage attracts resources, which produces more success and further advantage. The intervention is to rebalance resource allocation or create separate success tracks.

### Tragedy of the Commons

Actors each benefit from using a shared resource while the cost is distributed across all users. The intervention is to create governance, limits, information, or incentives that internalize the cost.

### Growth and Underinvestment

Growth creates demand for capacity, but delayed or insufficient investment lowers quality and suppresses future growth. The intervention is to invest before visible collapse and track capacity leading indicators.

## Application Steps

1. Describe the observed behavior over time.
2. Draw the reinforcing and balancing loops that could produce that behavior.
3. Match the loop structure to an archetype only if the causal topology fits.
4. Identify the usual high-leverage intervention for that archetype.
5. Test whether the intervention changes the loop or only adds another symptomatic fix.
6. Return the archetype, loop explanation, and intervention warning to the host mode.

## Detection Signals

- Growth stalls despite continued effort.
- Quick fixes repeatedly create delayed worsening.
- Standards quietly decline under pressure.
- Two actors are locked in reciprocal escalation.
- A shared resource is being depleted by individually rational behavior.
- Early winners keep receiving the resources that ensure they keep winning.

## Critical Questions

- What exact feedback loops make this archetype present?
- Is the pattern dynamic over time, or just a static resemblance?
- Where is the delay, constraint, or reinforcing comparison loop?
- What intervention changes the structure rather than the symptom?
- Which actor has the authority to change the rule or information flow?
- What evidence would show the archetype diagnosis is wrong?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Label substitution** - Detection: an archetype is named without mapping the loops. Correction: require the causal loop diagram before using the label.
- **Symptom addiction** - Detection: the analysis recognizes shifting the burden but still recommends the symptomatic fix. Correction: pair short-term relief with a concrete fundamental fix.
- **Constraint neglect** - Detection: limits to growth is diagnosed, but the limiting condition remains vague. Correction: name the specific scarce capacity, resource, tolerance, or bottleneck.
- **Actor flattening** - Detection: escalation or tragedy of the commons is described without actor-level incentives. Correction: map each actor's payoff and perception.
- **No-time-pattern error** - Detection: a one-time event is forced into an archetype. Correction: use archetypes only for behavior unfolding over time.
