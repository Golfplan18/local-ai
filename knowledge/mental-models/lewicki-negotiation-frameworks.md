# Lewicki Negotiation Frameworks

## Trigger

Invoked when a negotiation mode needs to supplement integrative Fisher-Ury analysis with the distributive/integrative continuum, bargaining range, concessions, and mixed-motive strategy. The host mode supplies the negotiation context; the lens supplies negotiation-structure diagnostics.

## Core Structure

Lewicki-style negotiation analysis treats negotiation as a mixed process that may contain both value creation and value claiming.

1. **Distributive bargaining.** Parties divide a fixed or perceived-fixed value; reservation points, targets, and concessions matter.
2. **Integrative negotiation.** Parties create value by uncovering interests, differences, trades, and joint gains.
3. **BATNA and reservation point.** Alternatives define walk-away power and the bargaining range.
4. **ZOPA.** The zone of possible agreement exists when reservation points overlap.
5. **Concession pattern.** The timing, size, and reciprocity of concessions signal expectations and power.
6. **Relationship and trust.** Information-sharing depends on trust, reputation, and future interaction.

## Application Steps

1. Identify whether the negotiation is primarily distributive, integrative, or mixed.
2. Estimate each side's BATNA, target, and reservation point where possible.
3. Determine whether a ZOPA exists or what information would reveal it.
4. Identify opportunities for value creation before value claiming.
5. Analyze concession strategy and information-sharing risk.
6. Return the negotiation structure and strategic caution for the host mode.

## Detection Signals

- Fisher-Ury analysis may be too optimistic about mutual gain.
- Price, scope, territory, or scarce resource division dominates the negotiation.
- Parties have hidden reservation points or asymmetric information.
- Concessions are doing strategic communication work.
- A context mixes relationship preservation with hard distributive stakes.

## Critical Questions

- Is there a real integrative possibility or mostly a distributive contest?
- What are each side's alternatives and reservation points?
- Does a ZOPA exist?
- What information can be shared safely, and what should be protected?
- Are concessions reciprocal, strategic, premature, or coerced?
- What relationship cost follows from hard bargaining?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Integrative overreach** - Detection: the analysis assumes joint gains where stakes are mostly distributive. Correction: name the fixed-pie component.
- **Distributive cynicism** - Detection: all negotiation is treated as zero-sum. Correction: look for differing priorities, forecasts, risk tolerance, and timing.
- **BATNA vagueness** - Detection: alternatives are rhetorical rather than concrete. Correction: specify the actual no-deal path.
- **ZOPA fantasy** - Detection: agreement is recommended without overlapping reservation points. Correction: identify the missing overlap or information gap.
