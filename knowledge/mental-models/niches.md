# Niches

## Trigger

Invoked from modes that analyze competitive positioning, market entry, or strategic differentiation — when a strategy of head-on competition is being weighed against a strategy of finding or building an unoccupied position. The host mode supplies the competitive landscape and the actor's capabilities; the lens supplies the niche-finding logic borrowed from evolutionary ecology.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

In ecology, a niche is the specific set of environmental conditions and resources that a species exploits. When two species compete for the same niche, one will eventually displace the other (the competitive exclusion principle). The strategic lesson: find or create an unoccupied position where competition is minimal and your particular strengths are maximally relevant. Winning is easier when you choose a game few others are playing.

### Mechanism

Competitive exclusion holds because two actors with identical resource needs in a finite resource pool cannot both grow indefinitely; one will out-compete the other along the dimension of marginal advantage. The corollary: when the resource demands or capability profiles differ enough, both can coexist by occupying distinct niches. Strategic positioning is the deliberate construction of capability differentiation that places the actor in an unoccupied or under-occupied niche.

### Applicability Conditions

- Competition is real and sustained, not transient.
- Capabilities differ across actors enough to support distinct positioning.
- The niche is large enough to sustain the actor at viable scale.
- The actor can credibly defend the niche against generalist encroachment.

### Common Misapplications

- Confusing a small market segment with a defensible niche; segments without barriers attract competitors quickly.
- Over-specializing into a niche that disappears when conditions shift.
- Building niche capabilities that have no demand (absence of competition is not the same as opportunity).
- Refusing to expand from a successful niche when conditions warrant it.

### Related Models

- **Red Queen Effect** — what happens when you stay in a contested niche: continuous improvement just to maintain position.
- **Differentiation Strategy** — Porter's strategic posture of which niche-finding is one tactic.
- **Competitive Exclusion Principle** — the underlying ecological mechanism.

### Worked example

A small law firm cannot compete with large firms on corporate M&A. Instead, it specializes in regulatory compliance for cryptocurrency exchanges — a niche too small and too novel for big firms to staff, but growing fast. The firm becomes the go-to expert, commands premium rates, and gets referrals from the very large firms that chose not to enter the space. By the time competitors notice, the firm has years of case experience and relationships that are expensive to replicate.

## Application Steps

1. Map the competitive landscape: who is serving whom, and where are the gaps?
2. Identify your unique combination of strengths — niches often exist at intersections.
3. Look for underserved segments: too small for large players, too specialized for generalists.
4. Validate the niche: confirm real demand exists, not just absence of supply.
5. Dominate the niche before expanding — depth of fit beats breadth of reach early on.
6. Watch for niche collapse: if conditions change or competitors arrive, be ready to adapt or move.

## Detection Signals

- Entering a crowded market or competitive field where incumbents are strong.
- Head-on competition is expensive and the odds of winning are low.
- A product, skill set, or offering does not fit neatly into existing categories.
- A generalist approach is producing mediocre results against focused competitors.
- Underserved segments are visible in customer feedback or unmet-need analyses.

## Critical Questions

- Is the niche large enough to sustain the actor at viable scale?
- Is the absence of competitors evidence of opportunity or evidence of no demand?
- Can the actor defend the niche when generalists notice it?
- Is the actor's capability differentiation real or merely claimed?
- What changes in environment would collapse the niche?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Empty niche fallacy** — assuming a gap in the market is a market gap. Detection: customer-discovery interviews reveal no actual demand. Correction: validate demand before committing capability.
- **Niche overfit** — building so narrowly into one niche that the actor cannot survive its disappearance. Detection: revenue concentration in a single use case. Correction: maintain optionality on adjacent niches.
- **Premature expansion** — leaving the niche before dominating it. Detection: the actor enters multiple niches at once and is mediocre in each. Correction: depth before breadth.
