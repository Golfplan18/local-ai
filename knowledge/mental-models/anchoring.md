# Anchoring

*A lens that explains how the first numerical or conceptual reference encountered on a topic disproportionately shapes all subsequent judgments, even when the anchor is arbitrary or known to be irrelevant.*

---

## Trigger

Invoked when the analyst observes a numerical estimate, valuation, or judgment that appears to have been disproportionately shaped by an initial figure, or when a negotiation outcome closely tracks the first offer made. The lens supplies the anchoring mechanism (insufficient adjustment from a salient initial value) and the diagnostic that anchoring is operating even when the anchor is arbitrary or explicitly disclaimed.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

The first piece of information encountered on a topic — the anchor — disproportionately shapes all subsequent judgments, even when the anchor is arbitrary or irrelevant. People adjust away from the anchor insufficiently, so the final estimate remains biased toward it. The effect is robust across domains and persists even when subjects know the anchor is random and are warned to disregard it.

### Mechanism

The cognitive system uses available information as a starting point for judgment and adjusts from it. Adjustment is effortful and stops when the estimate moves into a "plausible range" — but the plausible range itself is shifted by the anchor. The result is that the final estimate is biased toward the anchor in proportion to the anchor's salience and inversely to the analyst's confidence in independent estimation. Anchoring operates beneath conscious awareness; subjects are typically unaware that the anchor influenced their judgment and resist this attribution even when shown the data.

### Applicability Conditions

- A numerical or rankable judgment is being formed.
- A salient anchor (a number, valuation, or position) is available before or during the judgment.
- The judgment domain is one where the analyst lacks a strong independent reference (precise base rates, calibrated intuition, or external comparators).
- Time or attention pressure prevents thorough independent estimation.

### Common Misapplications

- Attributing every numerical judgment near a recently mentioned number to anchoring; coincidence is possible.
- Assuming awareness of the anchor neutralizes it; awareness reduces but does not eliminate the bias.
- Treating anchoring as a cognitive defect to be corrected; in negotiation, it is a tactical lever to be used as well as defended against.

### Related Models

- **Availability Heuristic** — the related shortcut that substitutes ease-of-recall for frequency.
- **Framing Effect** — the related bias where the wording rather than the content of an option shifts judgment.
- **Adjustment Heuristic** — the broader family of which anchoring is the dominant member.

## Application Steps

1. Identify the judgment being formed and the candidate anchors available to the analyst at the time.
2. Check whether the analyst's estimate is closer to the anchor than to an independently constructed reference value.
3. Construct a reference value from independent data (base rates, comparables, first-principles estimation) without consulting the anchor.
4. Compare the anchored and independent estimates; the gap measures the anchoring effect.
5. In negotiations, decide deliberately whether to make the first offer (anchor in your favor) or to absorb and explicitly counter the other party's anchor.

## Detection Signals

- An estimate is suspiciously close to a recently mentioned number with no analytical justification.
- A negotiation outcome closely tracks the first offer made, regardless of the other party's true reservation.
- A pricing page shows a high "original price" prominently next to the actual price.
- Performance reviews or forecasts cluster near last year's numbers without independent justification.
- The analyst defends an estimate by reference to a number they are aware is arbitrary.

## Critical Questions

- Was the anchor actually available before or during the judgment, or has the analyst attributed influence to a number that came afterward?
- Is the estimate's closeness to the anchor greater than chance, or could it reflect coincidence or genuine convergence on the right answer?
- Has the analyst constructed a reference value independent of the anchor, or only critiqued the anchored estimate without producing an alternative?
- Is the analyst aware of the anchor and explicitly attempting to correct, or is the bias operating beneath awareness? Awareness reduces but does not eliminate the effect.
- Is the situation one where the anchor might genuinely be informative (an expert valuation, a market price), or is the anchor arbitrary?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Anchor-attribution overreach** — every estimate near a salient number is diagnosed as anchored. Detection: the diagnosis is asserted regardless of evidence that the analyst constructed the estimate independently. Correction: require evidence that the anchor preceded and was salient during the judgment formation.
- **Awareness-as-cure illusion** — the analyst believes that knowing about anchoring neutralizes it. Detection: the analyst proceeds confidently without independent estimation. Correction: construct the independent reference value as a discipline regardless of awareness.
- **Anti-anchoring overshoot** — the analyst over-corrects and produces an estimate biased *away* from the anchor in the opposite direction. Detection: the corrected estimate has no independent justification for its level. Correction: ground the estimate in independent data, not in opposition to the anchor.
- **Negotiation passivity** — the analyst recognizes anchoring but fails to use it offensively when they have good information. Detection: the other party consistently anchors first and the analyst plays defense. Correction: when the analyst's information supports it, make the first offer to anchor in their favor.
