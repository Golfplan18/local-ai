# Forrester Industrial Dynamics

## Trigger

Invoked when a systems-dynamics mode needs the original industrial-dynamics frame for policy resistance, feedback, delays, and counterintuitive system behavior. The host mode supplies the organization, process, or supply system; the lens supplies Forrester's managerial systems lens.

## Core Structure

Industrial dynamics models organizations and industrial systems as feedback systems. Problems often arise not from isolated bad decisions but from the interaction of policies, delays, information flows, and accumulations.

1. **Policy structure.** Decision rules that managers or operators repeatedly apply.
2. **Information feedback.** What actors observe and how late or distorted it is.
3. **Stocks and flows.** Inventories, backlogs, capacity, cash, demand, staffing, or trust accumulate and drain.
4. **Delays.** Orders, hiring, production, learning, and perception take time.
5. **Amplification.** Local decisions can create oscillations, bullwhip effects, or instability.
6. **Counterintuitive behavior.** Obvious fixes can worsen the system because they act on symptoms.

## Application Steps

1. Identify the recurring decision policy.
2. Identify the information available to the decision-maker.
3. Map the key stocks, flows, feedback, and delays.
4. Ask how the policy behaves when feedback is late or distorted.
5. Look for oscillation, overshoot, amplification, and policy resistance.
6. Recommend a policy or information-flow change that improves system behavior.

## Detection Signals

- A business, operational, or supply process oscillates or overcorrects.
- Delays between action and feedback distort decisions.
- Local rational decisions produce global instability.
- A proposed fix pushes harder on the same policy that caused the problem.

## Critical Questions

- What repeated policy is generating behavior?
- What information does the actor use, and how delayed is it?
- Which stock is being managed indirectly?
- Where does amplification enter?
- What counterintuitive effect might the obvious fix produce?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Blame substitution** - Detection: instability is blamed on individuals without policy analysis. Correction: model recurring decision rules.
- **Delay blindness** - Detection: actors respond to stale information as if current. Correction: represent information delay.
- **Symptom pushing** - Detection: recommendations intensify the policy that creates oscillation. Correction: change feedback, delays, or rules.
- **No accumulation** - Detection: analysis ignores stocks such as backlog, capacity, or inventory. Correction: identify accumulations.
