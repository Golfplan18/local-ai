# Rumelt Strategy Kernel

## Trigger

Invoked when a mode needs to distinguish real strategy from aspirations, values, goals, priorities, or a loose list of initiatives. The host mode supplies the decision context, constraint set, or proposed plan; the lens supplies Rumelt's three-part kernel: diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent action.

## Core Structure

Good strategy has a kernel. The kernel does not need to be long, but it must make the situation intelligible, choose a governing approach, and align action around that approach.

### Component 1 - Diagnosis

The diagnosis names the critical challenge and simplifies the messy situation into the few forces that matter most. It is not a restatement of the goal. A diagnosis explains why the current situation is hard and what must be overcome.

Example: "Our support burden is rising because onboarding leaves users confused during the first three tasks" is a diagnosis. "Improve customer satisfaction" is not.

### Component 2 - Guiding Policy

The guiding policy is the overall approach for dealing with the diagnosis. It focuses attention and rules out tempting but incoherent alternatives. It is not yet a task list.

Example: "Make the first three tasks self-explanatory before adding new product surface area" is a guiding policy. It tells teams what kind of tradeoffs to make.

### Component 3 - Coherent Action

Coherent action is a coordinated set of steps that implements the guiding policy. The actions reinforce each other and are feasible under the constraints identified in the diagnosis.

Example: simplify first-run screens, add guided defaults, measure completion of the first three tasks, and defer unrelated feature work until onboarding completion rises.

## Application Steps

1. State the proposed goal, plan, or decision in plain language.
2. Extract the diagnosis: what is the real challenge, constraint, bottleneck, or asymmetry?
3. Test whether the diagnosis explains difficulty rather than merely restating desire.
4. Derive a guiding policy that follows from the diagnosis and makes tradeoffs explicit.
5. List coherent actions that implement the guiding policy.
6. Remove actions that do not reinforce the policy or that belong to a different diagnosis.
7. Return the strategy as diagnosis, guiding policy, and coherent action.

## Detection Signals

- A plan contains goals and initiatives but no explanation of the core challenge.
- Many priorities compete for attention and no principle chooses among them.
- A decision needs a strategic spine before comparing options.
- The proposed actions are individually reasonable but mutually disconnected.
- A critique needs to ask whether a strategy is actually a strategy.

## Critical Questions

- What is the diagnosis, and does it explain why the problem is hard?
- Does the guiding policy follow from the diagnosis?
- What tradeoffs does the guiding policy make explicit?
- Do the actions reinforce one another, or are they an accumulation of unrelated work?
- What tempting action is deliberately excluded by the strategy?
- Is the kernel specific enough that a team could use it to decide what not to do?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Goal masquerade** - Detection: the "strategy" is a desired outcome. Correction: force a diagnosis of the obstacle between current state and desired state.
- **Action pile** - Detection: many initiatives are listed without a governing policy. Correction: group actions by the policy they implement and remove the rest.
- **Template strategy** - Detection: the plan uses generic categories that fit any organization. Correction: name the specific challenge and asymmetry in this case.
- **Diagnosis evasion** - Detection: the strategy avoids the painful fact, bottleneck, or conflict. Correction: ask what the plan would have to admit if it were honest.
- **Incoherence** - Detection: actions optimize different theories of the problem. Correction: choose one diagnosis or explicitly split the problem into separate strategies.
