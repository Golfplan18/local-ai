# Shackel Motte and Bailey

## Trigger

Invoked when an argument may alternate between a modest defensible claim and a stronger desirable claim. The host mode supplies the argumentative artifact; the lens supplies the motte-and-bailey distinction and the evidence standard for using it without turning it into a loose insult.

## Core Structure

The **motte** is the defensible, limited claim an arguer retreats to under pressure. The **bailey** is the stronger, more attractive, more controversial claim the arguer advances when pressure is absent. The pattern matters when the defensibility of the motte is used to shield the appeal of the bailey.

Shackel's original usage is doctrinal: it characterizes a position that shifts between two versions of itself. Wider usage treats motte-and-bailey as a fallacy label for a single argumentative retreat. Ora should prefer the doctrinal reading when multi-claim evidence exists and explicitly mark narrower single-move usage when evidence is limited.

## Application Steps

1. Identify the ambitious claim that carries the argumentative payoff.
2. Identify the modest claim used when the ambitious claim is challenged.
3. Show the relationship between them: what meaning, prestige, or conclusion transfers from motte to bailey?
4. Check whether the arguer actually alternates between them.
5. Decide whether the evidence supports doctrinal structure or only a narrower fallacy-label use.
6. State the motte, the bailey, the alternation evidence, and the confidence level.

## Detection Signals

- The argument sounds reasonable when challenged but expansive when persuading.
- A critic attacks a strong claim and receives a defense of a weaker one.
- The defensible claim and the attractive claim share language but not scope.
- Multiple passages shift between modest and ambitious versions of the thesis.

## Critical Questions

- What exactly is the motte, and what exactly is the bailey?
- Where does the text move from one to the other?
- Is the bailey relying on the motte's defensibility?
- Is this a structural pattern across the argument or only one ambiguous sentence?
- Could charity reconstruct the claims as a single stable position?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Label slap** - Detection: the analysis says "motte and bailey" without quoting or paraphrasing both claims. Correction: name both claims and the transfer between them.
- **Scope confusion** - Detection: the motte and bailey differ only rhetorically, not substantively. Correction: show the difference in commitment.
- **Over-strict Shackel use** - Detection: a clear single retreat is ignored because it is not doctrinal. Correction: mark it as wider fallacy-label usage rather than doctrinal structure.
- **Under-charity** - Detection: the strongest consistent version of the argument is not considered. Correction: try a stable reconstruction before naming the pattern.
