# Force Field Analysis

## Trigger

Invoked from within change-management, stakeholder-analysis, intervention-design, and resistance-diagnosis modes when an analyst is planning a change initiative and needs to understand the structure of resistance, when a project is stalled and the blocking factors are unclear, or when the question is whether to push harder for change or to remove obstacles to it. The host mode supplies the desired change and its current state; the lens supplies the bilateral force-mapping protocol (driving forces vs. restraining forces) that makes invisible dynamics visible and reveals the highest-leverage interventions — typically weakening restraining forces rather than strengthening driving ones.

## Core Structure

**Input:** A desired change or end state, the current state, and access to the stakeholders or system whose dynamics will determine whether the change occurs.

**Output:** A bilateral force diagram with scored forces on both sides, an identified highest-leverage intervention, and a plan that targets the dominant restraining force.

1. **Define the change or desired state clearly.** Place it at the center of the diagram. Vague change definitions produce vague force lists. Input: the change initiative. Output: a one-sentence description of the desired end state.

2. **List all driving forces.** Reasons for the change, supporters, pressures, incentives, structural enablers. Place them on the left, pointing toward the change. Input: stakeholder input, organizational analysis. Output: a left-side force list.

3. **List all restraining forces.** Fears, costs, habits, opposing stakeholders, structural barriers, switching costs. Place them on the right, pointing away from the change. Input: stakeholder input, structural analysis. Output: a right-side force list.

4. **Score each force's relative strength (1-5).** Identify the dominant forces on both sides. The scoring is judgment-based but should be defended; weak scoring undermines the prioritization that follows. Input: the force lists. Output: scored force lists.

5. **Choose intervention strategy based on scoring.** Three options: strengthen a driving force, weaken a restraining force, or convert a restraining force into a driving one. The third is often most powerful when feasible. Reducing the strongest restraining force usually produces more movement than amplifying an already-strong driving force. Input: the scored lists. Output: an intervention selection with rationale.

6. **Plan the intervention against the chosen target.** Specify the actions, the resources required, and the expected reduction in force strength. Input: the intervention selection. Output: an intervention plan with measurable success criteria.

## Application Steps

1. Receive the desired change and the current state from the host mode.
2. Run the six-step protocol above.
3. Return the force diagram, the intervention selection, and the action plan to the host mode.
4. Flag any restraining force that cannot be weakened or converted as an acceptance-of-risk for the host mode to incorporate into its delivery plan.

## Detection Signals

- A change initiative has stalled and adding more pressure is not working.
- A decision has been made but implementation is meeting unexpected resistance.
- Multiple stakeholders have conflicting interests in the outcome.
- The need to prioritize battles is on the table — not all resistance is worth overcoming.
- A proposal seems obviously good but people are not adopting it.

## Critical Questions

- Have all material forces been identified, or is the analysis stuck on the visible ones?
- Are the force scores defensible with evidence (stakeholder statements, observed behavior, comparable cases), or are they impressionistic?
- Is the chosen intervention actually targeting the highest-leverage force, or is it the most comfortable to execute?
- Has resistance been analyzed as information about the system, or has it been treated as obstruction to be overcome?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Driver inflation** — Detection signal: the team responds to resistance by piling on additional driving forces (more arguments, more pressure). Correction: weaken or convert restraining forces; additional drivers compound resistance more than they overcome it.
- **Resistance-as-obstruction** — Detection signal: restraining forces are treated as opposition to be defeated rather than as information. Correction: read each restraining force as a signal about what the system requires for the change to be sustainable.
- **Comfortable-target selection** — Detection signal: the intervention targets the easiest force, not the highest-leverage one. Correction: rank by force strength and select the target by leverage, not by ease.
