# Devil's Advocacy

## Trigger

Invoked from within decision-stress-testing, groupthink-prevention, and plan-review modes when an analyst observes that a group is converging on a plan without systematic argument against it, especially when the cost of being wrong is high and the social environment penalizes dissent. The host mode supplies the plan or proposal under review; the lens supplies the role-assignment protocol that legitimates structured opposition, removing the social cost of dissent and forcing the group to engage with counter-arguments it would otherwise suppress.

## Core Structure

**Input:** A plan, decision, or proposal that has reached or is approaching commitment, with a group whose dissent has been thin or absent.

**Output:** A documented set of strongest-form objections to the plan, the group's responses to each, and a revised plan that addresses load-bearing objections.

1. **Appoint the devil's advocate explicitly.** Name the role, sanction it from the leader, and make clear that the advocate's critique is role-based rather than personal. Without explicit appointment, criticism remains optional and individual; the role's protective function depends on its formal status. Input: the plan and the group context. Output: a named advocate (or small team) with sanction.

2. **Provide the advocate full access and preparation time.** Give the advocate complete plan details, supporting data, and enough preparation time (typically days, not minutes) to construct a real case. Token preparation produces token critique. Input: the plan documents and supporting analysis. Output: the advocate's prepared case.

3. **Present the strongest possible case against the plan.** The advocate argues in good faith, not theatrically; the goal is to surface the actual best objections, not to perform opposition. Token objections discredit the role and let the group dismiss the exercise. Input: the prepared case. Output: a structured presentation of objections, ranked by severity.

4. **Group responds to each argument on its merits.** Each objection must be engaged substantively; dismissing the advocate or attacking the role defeats the purpose. The leader enforces the engagement norm. Input: the advocate's presentation. Output: a documented response to each objection (refutation, partial acceptance, or full acceptance).

5. **Revise the plan to address load-bearing objections.** Objections that the group cannot refute become required modifications to the plan. Document which objections drove which revisions. Input: the response set from step 4. Output: a revised plan with traceability to the critique.

6. **Rotate the role across meetings.** No one becomes the permanent critic; the skill spreads across the group; the role retains its protective character. Input: the team roster and meeting cadence. Output: a rotation schedule.

## Application Steps

1. Receive the plan and the group context from the host mode.
2. Run the six-step protocol above.
3. Return the revised plan and the documented critique-and-response record to the host mode.
4. Flag any objection that was raised but not addressed for explicit acceptance-of-risk by the decision-maker.

## Detection Signals

- A team is about to commit significant resources and has not formally challenged the plan.
- The decision environment punishes dissent — strong hierarchy, tight-knit culture, dominant leader.
- Prior decisions failed because known objections were never raised.
- A proposal passed quickly through review with no substantive pushback.
- The stakes are high enough that being wrong is far more costly than the discomfort of structured criticism.

## Critical Questions

- Has the role been explicitly appointed and sanctioned, or is it an implicit invitation that the advocate can decline?
- Was the advocate given enough preparation time and access to construct a real case, or was the exercise scheduled to fit a calendar slot?
- Did the group engage with each objection on its merits, or were objections dismissed by attacking the role or the advocate?
- Have load-bearing objections actually changed the plan, or has the exercise been performed without affecting the outcome?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Token critique** — Detection signal: the advocate raises only weak objections, signaling that the role is performative. Correction: appoint advocates known to have substantive concerns; require the strongest case.
- **Dismissive engagement** — Detection signal: the group attacks the advocate or the exercise rather than the objections. Correction: leader enforces substantive engagement; flag dismissive responses as procedural failures.
- **Permanent critic** — Detection signal: the same person fills the advocate role repeatedly and becomes identified as "the difficult one." Correction: rotate the role to spread the skill and protect any individual from the social cost.
