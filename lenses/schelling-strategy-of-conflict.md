# Schelling Strategy of Conflict

## Trigger

Invoked when strategic-interaction analysis involves threats, promises, bargaining, deterrence, credibility, focal points, or mixed-motive conflict. The host mode supplies the players and strategic situation; the lens supplies Schelling's commitment and coordination tools.

## Core Structure

Schelling treats conflict as bargaining under interdependence. Even adversaries share some interest in coordination, and strategic advantage often comes from shaping expectations.

1. **Mixed motives.** Actors may compete and coordinate at the same time.
2. **Credible commitment.** Binding oneself can make threats or promises believable.
3. **Threats and promises.** Conditional actions shape another actor's choice.
4. **Focal points.** Salient solutions coordinate expectations without communication.
5. **Brinkmanship.** Controlled risk of disaster can create bargaining pressure.
6. **The power to constrain oneself.** Removing options can strengthen bargaining position.

## Application Steps

1. Identify each actor's interests in conflict and coordination.
2. Identify threats, promises, commitments, and signals.
3. Test credibility: would the actor follow through?
4. Identify focal points or default expectations.
5. Assess whether risk, uncertainty, or brinkmanship is being used.
6. Recommend changes to commitment, communication, or focal structure.

## Detection Signals

- Actors bargain under threat or deterrence.
- Credibility matters more than stated preference.
- Coordination is possible but expectations are unstable.
- An actor benefits by limiting its own freedom.
- Escalation risk is used as leverage.

## Critical Questions

- What shared interest exists despite conflict?
- Which commitments are credible and why?
- What focal point would actors naturally select?
- Who benefits from ambiguity?
- Is risk being controlled or merely hoped away?
- What option could be removed to make a commitment credible?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Preference-only analysis** - Detection: stated desires are analyzed without credibility. Correction: test incentives to follow through.
- **Coordination neglect** - Detection: adversaries are treated as having no shared interest. Correction: identify the bargaining overlap.
- **Focal-point blindness** - Detection: salience and convention are ignored. Correction: ask what outcome actors expect others to expect.
- **Brinkmanship romance** - Detection: risk-taking is admired without control analysis. Correction: assess accident, misperception, and off-ramp risk.
