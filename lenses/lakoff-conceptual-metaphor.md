# Lakoff Conceptual Metaphor

## Trigger

Invoked from within `frame-audit` (T1) when that mode needs to identify the conceptual metaphors structuring an argument, narrative, or political artifact, and from within `frame-comparison` and `worldview-cartography` (T9) when those modes need to compare or map alternative metaphorical structurings of the same domain. The host mode supplies the artifact, the discourse, or the worldview to be analyzed; the lens supplies the cognitive-linguistic apparatus for surfacing the conceptual metaphors at work, the entailments those metaphors carry, and the alternative metaphors that would foreground different aspects of the target domain.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

Conceptual metaphor is not decorative language but cognitive structure: human reasoning about abstract or unfamiliar domains (the *target* domain) is largely structured by mappings from more concrete or familiar domains (the *source* domain). Metaphors like ARGUMENT IS WAR, TIME IS MONEY, LOVE IS A JOURNEY, or THE NATION IS A FAMILY are not just figures of speech — they shape what counts as a sensible inference, what is salient, what is foregrounded, and what is hidden. Because metaphors operate below conscious attention most of the time, they exert framing power that conscious deliberation does not check. Auditing an artifact's metaphorical infrastructure surfaces commitments that are doing inferential work without being argued for.

### Mechanism

A conceptual metaphor is a systematic mapping from a source domain to a target domain in which structure (entities, relations, inferential patterns) is carried over. ARGUMENT IS WAR maps combatants to disputants, attacks to objections, defenses to responses, victories to wins. The mapping licenses certain inferences ("she demolished his argument," "he had to retreat from his position") and obscures others (collaborative arguing as joint inquiry, where the mapping has no good slot). When an artifact's reasoning depends on the mapping's entailments, the metaphor is doing real cognitive work — work that survives even when the artifact never explicitly acknowledges the metaphor. Three properties are load-bearing for the analysis:

**Foregrounding and backgrounding.** Every conceptual metaphor highlights some features of the target while hiding others. ARGUMENT IS WAR foregrounds opposition and victory; it backgrounds shared inquiry and joint discovery. The political and analytical consequences of a metaphor are often best diagnosed by asking what it makes invisible, not by what it makes visible.

**Inferential transfer.** Inferences valid in the source domain are imported to the target without explicit argument. If WAR is zero-sum, ARGUMENT IS WAR makes argument zero-sum without anyone arguing the case. The transfer is automatic and largely unconscious; this is what makes it framing power.

**Multiple available mappings.** Most target domains can be structured by multiple alternative metaphors, each foregrounding different aspects. The choice of mapping is rarely innocent — it reflects (or constitutes) a stance on what about the target matters. ARGUMENT IS WAR vs. ARGUMENT IS COLLABORATIVE INQUIRY produces different inferential structures and different evaluations of the same exchange.

A frequently-cited application of this apparatus is Lakoff's analysis of American moral and political discourse in *Moral Politics*. Lakoff argues that two competing metaphorical models of THE NATION IS A FAMILY structure conservative and liberal moral reasoning respectively: the **strict-father model** (the family is hierarchical, the father provides discipline and authority, children become moral by internalizing punishment for disobedience) and the **nurturant-parent model** (the family is egalitarian, parents provide care and empathy, children become moral by internalizing the experience of being cared for and learning to extend that care). Each model, when applied to the nation, generates a coherent set of policy preferences and value judgments. *This application is one well-known instance of the underlying methodology, not the methodology itself.* The conceptual-metaphor apparatus applies far more generally — to any abstract or unfamiliar target domain that human reasoners structure via concrete-domain source mappings — and the political application is one (contested, partial) deployment of it. Lenses invoking this apparatus should not collapse "conceptual metaphor analysis" into "strict-father vs. nurturant-parent analysis"; the family-models analysis is a worked example, not the framework.

A frame, in the Lakoff/Fillmore sense, is the broader cognitive structure of which conceptual metaphors are one component. A frame organizes a domain by specifying its participants, their roles, the actions available, and the inferential expectations the domain carries. Activating a frame brings its full structure to bear on the topic at hand — including its metaphorical components. This is why the choice of vocabulary in political (and other persuasive) discourse is consequential: words activate frames, frames carry inferential structure, and the structure does work whether or not it is consciously processed.

### Applicability Conditions

- The host mode's target is an artifact, discourse, or worldview that operates in a target domain abstract enough or unfamiliar enough that metaphorical structuring is plausible (most political, moral, scientific-popularization, and explanatory discourse qualifies).
- The host mode needs to surface inferential commitments that are not explicitly argued for.
- The analytical purpose is to compare alternative framings or to make framing power visible, rather than to evaluate first-order factual claims.
- The artifact uses concrete vocabulary that maps onto an abstract target (suggesting a metaphor is at work), or its inferential moves are explicable only by reference to a metaphorical mapping.

### Common Misapplications

- Treating every metaphor as politically loaded. Some metaphors are politically inert (TIME IS MONEY structures time-management without obvious political work); the apparatus applies to them but yields no political diagnosis.
- Treating the strict-father / nurturant-parent analysis as the apparatus. This is one application; reducing the apparatus to it loses generality and miscarries when applied to non-political artifacts.
- Treating metaphor analysis as definitive worldview reconstruction. Metaphors are one component of cognitive structuring; treating a metaphor inventory as a complete worldview map overshoots the apparatus.
- Decorative-metaphor confusion. Not every linguistic metaphor reflects an underlying conceptual metaphor; one-off literary metaphors do not necessarily indicate cognitive structuring. The distinction requires attending to whether the metaphor's inferential transfers are doing systematic work in the artifact.
- Reading entailments mechanically. Metaphorical mappings are partial — not every source-domain entailment transfers, and identifying which transfers and which do not requires textual evidence, not deduction from the mapping.

### Related Models

- **Frame analysis (Goffman, Entman)** — the broader study of how cognitive frames structure interpretation; Lakoff's conceptual metaphor is one mechanism by which frames operate.
- **Stanley propaganda** — propaganda often works by activating frames whose metaphorical structure carries the propagandist's commitments without explicit argument.
- **Cappelen-Plunkett conceptual engineering** — when the engineering target is a concept partly structured by metaphor, the metaphorical infrastructure constrains what revisions are coherent.
- **Walton schemes** — the Argument from Analogy scheme is the dialectical surface of what conceptual metaphor often does cognitively.
- **Gallie essentially contested concepts** — many essentially contested concepts are contested precisely because their metaphorical structurings differ across factions; the contestation often runs at the metaphor level.

## Application Steps

1. Receive the artifact, discourse, or worldview from the host mode.
2. Identify the target domain(s) being structured.
3. Identify the source domain(s) being mapped onto the target — look for concrete vocabulary used in abstract contexts and for inferential moves that are explicable only by source-domain entailments.
4. Make the mapping explicit: list the entities, relations, and inferences that transfer from source to target.
5. Identify what the mapping foregrounds and what it backgrounds; ask what aspects of the target the mapping makes invisible.
6. Identify alternative metaphorical mappings that would structure the same target differently; note what each alternative would foreground and background.
7. Return the metaphor inventory, the foreground/background analysis, and the alternative mappings to the host mode.

## Detection Signals

- An artifact uses concrete vocabulary in abstract contexts (battle terms in arguments, journey terms in relationships, family terms in politics).
- Inferential moves in the artifact are explicable only by reference to source-domain entailments rather than by explicit argument.
- The host mode (frame-audit, frame-comparison, worldview-cartography) explicitly invokes this lens.
- Two parties disagree about a domain in ways that suggest they are operating with different metaphorical structurings rather than disagreeing about facts.
- Mid-analysis, the analyst notices that an artifact's persuasive force depends on importing source-domain entailments that would be controversial if argued for explicitly.

## Critical Questions

- Is the mapping actually doing inferential work in the artifact, or is the metaphor merely decorative? A metaphor that does not generate inferential transfer is linguistic ornament, not conceptual structuring.
- Have alternative metaphorical mappings been considered? Identifying only the metaphor present in the artifact, without naming alternatives, misses half the apparatus's analytical value.
- Is the foreground/background analysis evidence-based or speculative? The claim that metaphor M backgrounds aspect A of target T must be supported by inferential moves the metaphor enables and inferential moves it suppresses, not by intuition.
- Has the analysis avoided collapsing into the strict-father / nurturant-parent application? The political family-model analysis is one application; treating it as the apparatus produces miscarriage when applied to non-political domains.
- Are the mapping's transfers being identified accurately? Metaphorical mappings are partial; treating every source-domain entailment as transferred over-reads the apparatus.
- Is the analysis sensitive to the difference between conscious and unconscious framing? Lakoff's claim is that metaphorical structuring largely operates below conscious attention; an analysis that treats audiences as choosing among metaphors with full awareness misrepresents the mechanism.

## Common Failure Modes

- **Family-model collapse** — reducing the apparatus to strict-father / nurturant-parent analysis. Detection: the analysis's metaphorical inventory is dominated by family-model variations regardless of the artifact's domain. Correction: apply the underlying methodology (identify target, identify source, identify mapping, identify entailments) without prejudging the source domain.
- **Decorative-metaphor over-reading** — treating every metaphor as cognitively load-bearing. Detection: the analysis attributes inferential work to metaphors that the artifact does not actually trade on. Correction: require evidence that the metaphor's entailments are doing inferential work in the artifact (specific moves the metaphor licenses or obscures).
- **Foreground-background speculation** — claiming that a metaphor backgrounds aspect A without textual evidence. Detection: the foreground/background claim cannot be cashed out in specific inferential moves enabled or suppressed. Correction: ground each foreground/background claim in a specific inferential move the metaphor enables (foreground) or fails to enable (background).
- **Alternative-blind analysis** — identifying the metaphor present in the artifact without naming alternatives. Detection: the analysis reads as if the artifact's metaphor is the only one that could structure the domain. Correction: actively generate at least one alternative mapping and analyze its different foreground/background structure.
- **Mechanism-blind reading** — describing what the metaphor does without attending to how it works (unconscious cognitive structuring vs. conscious choice). Detection: the analysis treats audiences as selecting among metaphors with full deliberation. Correction: distinguish the cognitive-structuring claim (largely unconscious) from claims about deliberate framing (which is conscious but also draws on the unconscious infrastructure).
