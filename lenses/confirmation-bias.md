# Confirmation Bias

## Trigger

Invoked from within bias-audit, hypothesis-testing, and evidence-evaluation modes when an analyst observes that evidence collection or interpretation appears to be running a rigged search — turning up support for a pre-existing view while disconfirming evidence is dismissed as outlier or methodologically flawed. The host mode supplies the hypothesis, claim, or position under evaluation; the lens supplies the diagnostic and corrective protocol that exposes one-sided search and forces equal-scrutiny treatment of confirming and disconfirming evidence.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

Once a belief is held, the mind unconsciously seeks evidence that supports it, interprets ambiguous data in its favor, and discounts or ignores what contradicts it. The error is not having beliefs — beliefs are unavoidable — but that the mind runs a one-sided search algorithm without the believer noticing. The stronger the emotional or identity investment in the position, the stronger the filter.

### Mechanism

Three sub-processes generate the bias. Selective search: when looking for evidence, the mind generates queries that would confirm the belief and neglects queries that would refute it. Selective interpretation: ambiguous evidence is read as supportive when the belief is already held. Selective recall: confirming instances are remembered more readily than disconfirming ones, distorting the evidence base in subsequent reflection. The processes operate below conscious awareness, which is why insiders to a debate cannot detect their own confirmation bias even after being informed of the concept.

### Applicability Conditions

- A specific hypothesis or belief is being evaluated.
- The evaluator has prior commitment to one outcome, even if mild.
- Evidence-gathering has been led by the evaluator rather than imposed by an independent source.
- The decision under consideration is high-stakes enough that being wrong is costly.

### Common Misapplications

- Diagnosing confirmation bias whenever someone reaches a conclusion the analyst disagrees with — without showing that the search was actually one-sided. Disagreement is not evidence of bias.
- Using the lens to dismiss converging evidence by claiming any agreement must be confirmation bias. Convergence from independent sources is a strength, not a symptom.

### Related Models

- **Motivated Reasoning** — the broader category; confirmation bias is the search-and-interpretation symptom.
- **Devil's Advocacy** — the structural counter-protocol that forces the missing disconfirming search.
- **Falsifiability** — Popper's criterion that operationalizes the corrective: state in advance what evidence would refute the belief.

## Application Steps

1. State the current belief or hypothesis explicitly in writing.
2. Define in advance what evidence would change the mind and where it would be found.
3. Actively assign disconfirming search to a specific person or process; in groups, designate a devil's advocate.
4. Apply equal scrutiny criteria to confirming and disconfirming evidence — the same methodological standards must be used.
5. Update belief proportionally to the strength of new evidence rather than to its emotional valence.

## Detection Signals

- The evaluator favors a hypothesis already and the search is being led by the evaluator.
- A team discussion is reinforcing a single conclusion with no substantive pushback.
- Evidence-gathering feels easy and comfortable; nothing is challenging the view.
- The first instinct on encountering counterevidence is to find flaws in it rather than to assess its weight.
- The phrase "every fact fits the theory" appears — a warning that the theory may be unfalsifiable as currently held.

## Critical Questions

- Has the evaluator stated in advance what evidence would refute the belief? If not, the search cannot be evaluated for one-sidedness.
- Is the disconfirming search being conducted with the same effort as the confirming search? If only the confirming search has been resourced, the bias is operative regardless of intent.
- Does the criticism of disconfirming evidence apply equally to confirming evidence of the same methodological type? If the standards differ, that asymmetry is the bias's signature.
- Could the apparent confirmation be explained by independent convergence of high-quality evidence rather than biased search? If yes, the bias diagnosis is weakened.

## Common Failure Modes

- **Disagreement-as-bias** — Detection signal: the lens is invoked whenever the analyst reaches a conclusion the critic disagrees with. Correction: require the critic to demonstrate one-sided search structure, not just disagreement with output.
- **Asymmetric application** — Detection signal: confirmation bias is named in opponents but never in oneself. Correction: run the lens on one's own most-held belief first.
- **Search-cost rationalization** — Detection signal: the team claims it lacked time to seek disconfirming evidence and so could not have run a balanced search. Correction: time-boxed disconfirming search (even 30 minutes) outperforms zero disconfirming search; the corrective is procedural, not exhaustive.
