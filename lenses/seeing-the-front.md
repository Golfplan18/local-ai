# Seeing the Front

## Trigger

Invoked from modes that evaluate leadership decisions, audit organizational reporting integrity, or analyze the gap between executive perception and operational reality — when reports, dashboards, or summaries are substituting for direct observation. The host mode supplies the leader, the decision domain, and the reporting chain; the lens supplies the direct-observation discipline that calibrates the mental model against ground truth.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

The quality of a decision degrades with distance from the front line. Reports are filtered, metrics are gamed, intermediaries soften bad news, and dashboards compress reality into numbers that obscure what actually matters. The best leaders go to the source — the factory floor, the customer call, the deployment pipeline, the sales meeting — and observe directly. This is not micromanagement; it is information integrity. You cannot lead what you cannot see.

### Mechanism

Each layer between the front and the leader applies filtering: aggregation, summarization, selective emphasis, and self-protective omission. Aggregated data loses the variance that contains the most important information. Summaries carry the framing of the summarizer. By the time information reaches the leader, signal has been compressed, anomalies smoothed, and bad news softened. Direct observation bypasses the chain and provides ground-truth calibration that filtered reports cannot.

### Applicability Conditions

- The leader makes decisions affecting work that happens at some distance.
- Reports and intermediaries exist between leader and front.
- The cost of wrong decisions is material.
- Direct observation is feasible (the front can be visited, watched, or listened to).

### Common Misapplications

- Treating direct observation as a substitute for systematic data rather than a complement.
- Using "front visits" as theater that does not produce learning.
- Micromanaging from the front rather than calibrating mental models.
- Disrupting the work being observed in ways that prevent honest observation.

### Related Models

- **Sensemaking** — what front observation enables when filtered reports have failed.
- **Procedural Justice** — what visible leadership presence at the front contributes to.
- **Information Asymmetry** — the structural property direct observation reduces.

### Worked example

A VP of Engineering reviews sprint velocity charts and concludes the team is on track. But spending one day sitting with the team reveals: velocity is maintained by reclassifying bugs as features, tech debt is silently growing, and two senior engineers are close to quitting because of on-call burden. None of this appeared in the dashboard. The charts measured output; the front visit revealed sustainability. The VP restructures on-call and adds a tech-debt sprint.

## Application Steps

1. Identify the front — where does the actual work, transaction, or interaction happen?
2. Go there physically or as close to it as possible — read raw support tickets, sit in on sales calls, watch deployments, talk to end users.
3. Observe without filtering — resist the urge to only see what confirms the current strategy.
4. Compare what you see with what the reports say — note the gaps.
5. Use the ground truth to calibrate your mental model, then adjust strategy accordingly.

## Detection Signals

- Decisions are being made based on secondhand reports, dashboards, or slide decks.
- There is a persistent gap between what leadership believes and what frontline workers experience.
- A situation is deteriorating and the data does not explain why.
- A leader is new to a domain and needs to build accurate mental models quickly.
- Strategy is reported successful but operational signals contradict it.

## Critical Questions

- Is the observation honest, or does the leader's presence distort the work?
- Has the leader observed multiple sites or only the showcase one?
- Is the gap between reports and observation a fluke or a pattern?
- Does the leader have time and patience to observe long enough for the front's actual rhythm to emerge?
- Are observations being used for calibration or for micromanagement?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Showcase observation** — the front is staged for the visit. Detection: observed work differs from work performed when the leader is absent. Correction: schedule unannounced or sustained observation.
- **Confirmation observation** — leader sees only what supports the existing strategy. Detection: the visit produces no surprises. Correction: explicitly seek disconfirming observations.
- **Micromanagement creep** — front observation becomes intervention. Detection: front teams adapt to the leader's preferences in real time. Correction: separate observation from direction; act on observations later.
