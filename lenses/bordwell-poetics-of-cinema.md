# Bordwell Poetics of Cinema

## Trigger

Invoked when visual-composition modes analyze a film still, cinematic sequence, mise-en-scene, or shot design. The host mode supplies the image or sequence; the lens supplies a poetics frame: how formal choices organize viewer attention, comprehension, affect, and inference.

## Core Structure

Bordwell's poetics asks how films are made to produce effects. It connects craft choices to viewer activity instead of treating style as decoration.

1. **Narrational function.** What information does the shot reveal, delay, hide, or emphasize?
2. **Compositional attention.** How do framing, blocking, light, movement, and depth guide the eye?
3. **Stylistic system.** How does this choice fit the film's broader pattern of cutting, staging, color, sound, or camera movement?
4. **Viewer inference.** What does the viewer have to infer from the arrangement?
5. **Historical convention.** Which genre, period, or craft convention is being used, revised, or violated?

## Application Steps

1. Identify the cinematic unit: still, shot, scene, or sequence.
2. Describe the formal features without interpretation first.
3. Ask what each feature does for attention, information, emotion, or inference.
4. Relate the feature to the surrounding sequence or expected convention.
5. State the compositional effect and the evidence for it.

## Detection Signals

- A visual analysis involves film, mise-en-scene, shot scale, camera placement, or editing.
- Composition appears to manage attention or narrative inference.
- The question is not just "what does this image mean?" but "how does this image work?"
- A still needs to be read as part of cinematic form rather than as an isolated picture.

## Critical Questions

- What viewer activity does this formal choice invite?
- What is emphasized, deferred, hidden, or made legible?
- Is the effect produced by framing, staging, lighting, editing, movement, or their combination?
- Does the analysis respect the sequence context?
- Is an art-historical claim being made where a cinematic claim is needed?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Symbol-only reading** - Detection: composition is reduced to meaning without mechanism. Correction: explain how form guides attention or inference.
- **Still-image flattening** - Detection: a film still is analyzed without sequence context. Correction: state what is known or unknown about before/after shots.
- **Technique inventory** - Detection: features are listed without effect. Correction: connect each feature to viewer experience.
- **Intent overreach** - Detection: claims about the director's intent exceed the evidence. Correction: ground claims in observable formal function.
