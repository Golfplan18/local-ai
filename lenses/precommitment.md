# Precommitment

## Trigger

Invoked from modes that design decision processes for an actor whose future self will face predictable temptation, pressure, or weakness of will — diet, investment, negotiation, organizational discipline. The host mode supplies the present decision and the predicted future weakness; the lens supplies the binding mechanism that constrains the future self.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

Bind your future self to a course of action before the moment of temptation arrives. A rational agent who knows they will face predictable weakness of will can improve outcomes by eliminating or restricting future choices. The archetype is Odysseus ordering his crew to tie him to the mast and ignore his pleas so he could hear the Sirens without steering into the rocks. Precommitment acknowledges that the self who plans and the self who executes are not the same agent — and designs constraints accordingly.

### Mechanism

Time-inconsistent preferences mean the present self values an outcome differently than the future self will. The present self can see the temptation pattern clearly because the affect is distant; the future self will be in the affect. Rather than relying on willpower (which is depleted exactly when needed), the present self constructs structural barriers that make defection costly, slow, or impossible. The future self remains free in everyday matters but is bound on the specific axis of predicted weakness.

### Applicability Conditions

- The future temptation or pressure is predictable in advance.
- The present self has accurate insight into the future failure mode.
- A binding mechanism is available that will hold under the predicted pressure.
- The cost of being bound exceeds the cost of free defection.

### Common Misapplications

- Binding the future self to a course of action that may need revision when conditions change.
- Choosing a binding mechanism too weak to hold under the actual pressure.
- Over-binding into rigidity, eliminating legitimate adaptation.
- Confusing precommitment with mere intention or stated resolve.

### Related Models

- **Hyperbolic Discounting** — the time-inconsistency mechanism that makes precommitment necessary.
- **Brinkmanship** — strategic precommitment to credibly limit one's own options.
- **Ulysses Contracts** — the formalized version of personal precommitment.

### Worked example

An investor decides to hold a diversified portfolio through market downturns. Knowing that a 30% drop will trigger panic selling (it always has before), they set up a precommitment: the portfolio is in a structure that requires a 48-hour cooling-off period and a written justification reviewed by their advisor before any sell order executes. When the next crash arrives, the investor feels the overwhelming urge to sell — but the structural delay forces a pause. By the time the 48 hours pass, the panic has subsided.

## Application Steps

1. Identify the future scenario where temptation or pressure will push you off course.
2. Design a binding mechanism — contractual, structural, social, or financial — that makes deviation costly or impossible.
3. Implement the mechanism now, while your judgment is clear and uncompromised.
4. Communicate the commitment to others so social accountability reinforces the structural constraint.
5. Accept that when the pressure arrives, you will want to break the commitment — that is the point; the mechanism holds when willpower would not.

## Detection Signals

- A decision made calmly today will face emotional pressure to reverse later (markets, diets, negotiations).
- An organization has a pattern of abandoning long-term strategy when short-term pain arrives.
- Negotiations are ahead and you need to credibly limit your own flexibility to strengthen your position.
- Time-inconsistent preferences are predictable — you know exactly when and how you will be tempted to defect.
- The cost of deviation is high but the temptation to deviate will feel justified in the moment.

## Critical Questions

- Is the predicted future failure mode actually likely, or is the analyst overestimating it?
- Will the chosen binding mechanism hold under the actual pressure, or is it weaker than imagined?
- Is the binding too rigid to accommodate legitimate condition changes?
- Does the precommitment require third-party enforcement, and is that party reliable?
- Has the analyst designed an escape clause for true emergencies that the future self can use without abusing?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Weak binding** — the mechanism is nominally restrictive but easily circumvented under pressure. Detection: when the pressure arrives, the mechanism is bypassed. Correction: design with adversarial future self in mind; assume strong willpower-defeating motivation.
- **Over-rigid binding** — legitimate condition changes can no longer be accommodated. Detection: the mechanism produces bad outcomes when the future world differs from predicted. Correction: include narrowly-defined override conditions reviewed by a third party.
- **Self-deception precommitment** — stating an intention as if it were a binding mechanism. Detection: no actual structural barrier exists. Correction: distinguish stated resolve from binding mechanism; build the latter.
