# Fundamental Attribution Error

## Trigger

Invoked when an analysis explains behavior, failure, conflict, or performance primarily through personal character while giving too little weight to the situation, constraints, incentives, information, or system pressures surrounding the actor. The host mode supplies the behavior or failure under investigation; the lens forces a character-versus-situation comparison before blame or cause is assigned.

## Core Structure

### Core Insight

People tend to overattribute other people's behavior to stable character and underattribute it to situational pressure. The same observer who explains another person's missed deadline as laziness may explain their own missed deadline by pointing to conflicting demands, unclear requirements, or broken dependencies.

### Mechanism

Behavior is vivid and the surrounding situation is often invisible. Observers see the action but not the pressures that made it likely: incentives, constraints, missing information, role expectations, workload, fatigue, authority gradients, or system design. The mind fills that visibility gap with character narratives because they are simpler and more emotionally satisfying than reconstructing context.

### Applicability Conditions

- A person or group is being judged from limited behavioral evidence.
- The explanation relies on traits such as lazy, careless, incompetent, hostile, or unreliable.
- Relevant situational pressures may be missing from the analysis.
- The decision following the judgment affects blame, discipline, hiring, promotion, conflict resolution, or root-cause repair.

### Common Misapplications

- Excusing genuinely repeated harmful behavior by overcorrecting into situation-only explanations.
- Treating situational analysis as moral absolution rather than causal accuracy.
- Applying the lens only to sympathetic actors while character-blaming disliked actors.
- Listing possible situational factors without investigating which ones actually applied.

### Related Models

- **Actor-Observer Bias** — related asymmetry between explanations of self and others.
- **Just Culture** — organizational safety frame that separates human error, at-risk behavior, and reckless behavior.
- **Incentives** — structural rewards and punishments that can produce behavior mistaken for character.

## Application Steps

1. State the current character attribution in plain form: "They did X because they are Y."
2. List plausible situational explanations that could produce the same behavior in a reasonable actor.
3. Identify which situational factors are evidence-backed, not merely possible.
4. Compare the pattern: single event, repeated behavior across contexts, or repeated behavior only under one constraint.
5. Separate causal diagnosis from accountability: what explains the behavior, and what response is still warranted?
6. Return the revised cause model and any system-level fixes that follow from it.

## Detection Signals

- A root-cause analysis ends at "human error," "bad attitude," "carelessness," or "incompetence."
- A performance review focuses on traits while ignoring workload, incentives, unclear authority, or broken dependencies.
- Conflict analysis assumes malicious intent from a single observed action.
- A process failure is assigned to the person closest to the failure rather than the conditions that shaped their choices.
- Different standards are applied to in-group and out-group actors.

## Critical Questions

- What situational pressures would produce this same behavior in a reasonable person?
- Is there a repeated cross-context pattern, or only behavior under one set of constraints?
- What information did the actor have at the time, and what information is only available in hindsight?
- Are incentives, role expectations, workload, or authority gradients doing causal work?
- What evidence would justify retaining a character-based explanation after situational factors are considered?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Character shortcut** - Detection: the explanation stops at a trait label. Correction: require at least three plausible situational causes before assigning disposition.
- **Situation-only overcorrection** - Detection: repeated harmful behavior is excused without accountability. Correction: separate explanation from responsibility and look for cross-context recurrence.
- **Hindsight contamination** - Detection: the actor is blamed for not using information they did not have. Correction: reconstruct the actor's information state at decision time.
- **Nearest-person blame** - Detection: the person closest to the failure absorbs blame for upstream design defects. Correction: trace constraints, handoffs, incentives, and dependencies.
